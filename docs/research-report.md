## 1. Abstract

We present a modular decision engine for BTC/USDT scalping on 30s–5m
bars, structured as ten composable layers: market data, feature
engineering, regime detection, dynamic support/resistance structure,
three specialist experts, an opportunity gate, a router, an independent
risk layer, execution simulation, and a feedback loop. The central
hypothesis is that **separating perception, opportunity assessment,
strategy selection, and risk control outperforms a monolithic predictor
under regime shifts** — and that this can be demonstrated (or refuted)
through a component-wise ablation ladder rather than claimed.

## 2. Problem

Single predictors overfit one regime, conflate confidence with
correctness, and lack an independent veto. We formalize the requirements:
causality (no look-ahead), veto-capable safety, regime adaptivity,
per-decision explainability, and reproducibility on laptop-class hardware.

## 3. System Architecture

Pipeline order (fixed, tested): `MarketSnapshot → FeatureVector →
RegimeState → ZoneMap → ExpertOutputs → OpportunityScore →
RouterDecision → RiskDecision → ExecutionRecord → SystemState`.
Regime is computed before structure within each bar. See
[`explain/architecture.md`](explain/architecture.md) and
[`design-decisions/`](design-decisions/README.md) (ADRs 1–6).

## 5. Feature Engineering

Config-driven periods (no magic numbers): momentum/volatility at
short/medium/long horizons, RSI, MACD, SMA(9/21), 5-bar returns,
orderflow imbalance proxy, volume z-score, plus structural features
(distance/strength to nearest zones) merged back into the vector.
Schema versioned (`FEATURE_SCHEMA_VERSION`); loaders fall back to
rule-based mode on mismatch. Causality is pinned by
`tests/unit/test_no_lookahead.py`.

## 6. Regime Detection

3-state Gaussian HMM over (volatility z, momentum z, volume z) with a
panic-spike override, distilled to LightGBM for serving; persistence
window suppresses flapping. Regime gates experts (candidate admission),
the opportunity filter, and the risk layer — each independently, for
defense in depth.

## 7. Expert Models

Trend, mean-reversion, breakout specialists. Each emits
`ExpertOutput(action, confidence, expected_return/risk,
invalidation_distance)`; training data is harvested from the zone
touch-event stream and labeled by triple-barrier. Fallback to
rule-based logic when models are absent/mismatched — the system never
requires a model to trade safely.

## 8. Router

LightGBM ranker over expert confidences + switching-context features,
blended with raw confidences; `no_trade` is a first-class candidate.
Current baseline comparison: naive highest-raw-confidence router.

## 9. Risk Model

Risk-based sizing (equity × risk_pct ÷ stop distance), position cap,
liquidity-scaled reduction, five persisted circuit breakers, trade rate
limits, kill switch. Hard rules veto ML absolutely (ADR-0004).

## 10. Experimental Setup

Walk-forward with purge + embargo (`WalkForwardSplitter`); the full-stack
harness runs one continuous causal replay (same code path as live),
segmented into non-overlapping evaluation windows; metrics computed on
the realized trade sequence, net of all costs. Baselines: buy & hold,
simple momentum, naive-confidence router, ungated filter. Ablations:
single-expert, risk-layer-off, regime-gating-off.

## 15. Future Work

sequence models behind the expert contracts; per-fold refitting in the
harness; WebSocket ingestion; multi-symbol partitioning; multi-week
paper-trading campaign as the gate to any live claim.
